The decision

Should Ascend IQ ship?

Decision: HOLD. I recommend we hold Ascend IQ's enterprise launch until it passes its release gate, because it overstates its sources in 6 of 20 audited answers, and one overstated fact repeated by a VP puts a $50k+ renewal at risk.

Module 6
Ship-or-hold memo
The arguments

1. Ascend IQ would break the verified-data promise our clients pay for. Evidence: 6 of 20 audited answers (30%) state facts their sources don't support, such as an invited speaker listed as confirmed. The release bar is 0 in 100 held-out questions. 2 other answers quote a wrong price or reverse a comparison, and even the most lenient judge tried fails 9 of 20.
2. A staged rollout wouldn't contain the risk. Evidence: The rollout's first 5 accounts are top-50 clients at $50k+ a year each, so the rollout alone would expose at least $250k a year of renewals to a build that fails its release gate. The staged rollout was planned for the latency gate, not for facts.
3. The hold has a clear exit: the check works, and only its test set is missing. Evidence: On 30 fresh answers the judge caught 13 of 13 overstatements with 1 false alarm in 17 (5.9%), inside the spec's bar of 95% caught and 6% false alarms. The sample is small, so the judge has to hold that bar again on the golden set, and none of its 100 held-out questions or 30 regression cases exist yet.

The evidence

Five modules of evidence

Module 1
Evaluation strategy
User promise

For VP-level strategists at our top 50 accounts, Ascend IQ promises answers in seconds where every figure traces back to Ascend's verified data, so that they make roadmap calls on Ascend and renew their $50k+ contracts.

Three trust metrics

Hallucination rate, because clients pay $50k+ a year for verified data, and one fabricated figure in a board pack undermines what they pay for. Robustness (calibrated refusal), because questions outside Ascend's data are where Ascend IQ will make things up. Latency, because slow answers are the complaint Ascend IQ exists to fix.

Trade-offs

Hallucination rate ↔ Latency: We prioritise grounded answers over response time because one fabricated competitor price in a VP's board pack costs more than a few seconds' wait, and a grounded answer is still far faster than digging through the platform by hand.
Robustness ↔ Latency: We prioritise robustness over latency because checking whether Ascend holds verified data before answering adds time, and a few extra seconds to say "we don't have verified data on that" costs far less than a confident answer about a company outside our coverage.

Module 3
Eval suite and spec
What the checks caught

On the 20 audit answers, the rule check and the judge each caught 6 of 6 inflated facts with no false alarms. On 30 fresh answers, the rule caught 4 of 13 with 5 false alarms in 17, and the judge caught 13 of 13 with 1 in 17. So the judge is the release gate, and the rule is a free early warning. That shows the judge can catch the failure; only the golden set can show the build is ready.

Eval spec

Target risk: inflated facts, where an answer says more than its source does. Evaluator: an LLM judge from a different model family, one answer per call. Threshold: zero confirmed in at least 100 held-out questions, with the judge at 95% or more caught, 6% or fewer false alarms and κ 0.6 or above. Owner: the Group PM, with the engineering lead owning the CI gate.

Module 4
Eval gates
Gate map

Row 1 wrong price: Hard, Pull Request. Row 5 unconfirmed speaker: Hard, Release. Row 8 false competitor comparison: Hard, Staging. Row 3 slow answer (4.2 s against a 2.0 s target): Soft, Staging. Row 17 slang: Advisory, Pull Request. Row 14 required legal refusal: no gate.

Release criteria

Hard: 0 confirmed inflated answers in 100 or more held-out questions, at Release. Soft: p95 latency of 2.0 s or less, at Staging. Advisory: 0% slang, on every pull request. If the Soft gate fails: a staged rollout to 5 of the top 50 accounts. No lever ships around the failed Hard gate.

Module 5
Coverage and budget
Coverage gaps

AI Report Summaries: hallucination covered, latency partial, and gaps on fairness, UX trust and robustness. Fairness accepted, and measured through the new judge's failure rate by sector and region. Robustness closed first, with a completeness judge on every summary before it's sent, because a summary that drops its key finding reads as well as a good one.

Eval budget

Online evals for data fabrication and overconfident tone, the guesses stated as facts behind my HOLD: $150,000 of the $200,000 cap. A weekly audit for context specificity, a fortnightly audit for data bias, and a timer for slow answers.

Insights

What I learned

Where the course was hardest, what you’ll keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

Trusting my own audit. Different judges failed between 9 and 15 of the same 20 answers, so I had to show the result didn't depend on how strict I was. 4 of the 6 inflated facts fail under every judge, and the bar is zero, so the HOLD stands whichever judge you trust.

Key learnings

I'll test every check on answers it wasn't written from before it gates anything. I'll gate each check separately, never on one blended score. I'll rank failures by whether the user can see them, and pressure-test each call with a different model family.

Aha moment

My word rule caught 6 of 6 inflated facts in the audit, then 4 of 13 on fresh answers. A check that passes the cases it was written from proves nothing. That made the judge, not the rule, the release gate.
